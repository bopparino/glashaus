## Design Specificity Verdict

**Specific at the top of one page; category-interchangeable on the four pages the visitor actually works in.**

Unrepeatable and genuinely authored: the drawn-empty slot (`NOTHING HELD` with a dashed rule; `— · — / NO DECISION`) is a move that only makes sense because of a product refusal — silence is the usual output, so an omitted row would be the wrong drawing. The content model is equally un-copyable: *what she went to sleep wanting*, *what she is chasing · not for you*, *the aperture is hers. you see that she thinks, not what she thinks*. No neighbouring product has a column for what the software wants that isn't for you. `DAY 290` as an inverted platform numeral and one ink reserved for a person rather than a status are real.

Not specific: strip the words and the kit is Archivo, flat white cards with 1px borders, a navy accent, uppercase 10.5px micro-labels, square corners, no shadow, mono numerals — the default "we are serious" template of the last three years. The Helsinki/transit-wayfinding lineage DESIGN.md claims is asserted but not drawn: no route-line vocabulary, no destination-board typography. Literal signage borrowings number two.

The decisive evidence: **the rail — documented as "the page's spine" — exists on Today and on no other page.** The drawn-empty slot, "the system's atom," likewise. `/memory` is a bare table with a native checkbox and zero headings; `/system` is key/value rows plus a `<pre>` log tail; `/journal` labels in a third grammar (`[ Dream ]`); `/self` prints its ten values twice. In Operate mode that is where the visitor lives.

**The second stated failure mode — "if the ornament goes decorative" — has NOT happened.** All three claims verified in source and on screen: `fingerprint()` prints eight live counts; `stampSVG()` seeds an LCG from those counts so density tracks accumulation; `sparkSVG()` draws rails at exactly 0.05/0.95, the real EWMA bounds. The claim holds. But it is honest and mute — nothing says the fingerprint is state, the rails carry no axis labels, and the fingerprint is `display:none` below 900px, hidden on the primary device.

## Overall Impression

This is a confident, unusually principled interface with a hole where its failure states should be. The first viewport of Today is the best thing in the project — `DAY 290`, the brass `01 · HER NIGHT` tag, and her epigraph at poster scale deliver the entire thesis in one screen. Then you navigate, and the program quietly stops: five of six pages are a competent admin panel wearing the same typeface.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. It is that **every frightening moment on this surface is unattended.** Forgetting a fact, a failing backup, a 500, a model that never answers — each one is unconfirmed, unexplained, or unfixable without a terminal the product promised was over after setup. The design has an excellent vocabulary for stating facts and none at all for *you're okay*.

## What's Working

**1. The drawn-empty slot.** `NOTHING HELD` with a dashed rule spanning the remaining width; `— · — / NO DECISION` in the timetable. It turns the product's most-defended refusal into a visible reading rather than a gap. Structurally unavailable to a neighbouring product — the single best argument against "any AI product could use this."

**2. The failure grammar.** One reservation — red is only ever a filled plate with inverted text — pays for itself immediately. On `/system` the failing `BACKUP` row is unmissable among nine passing ones, and because red is never spent elsewhere, civic blue carries all structure without the page reading as an alarm. Holds in both plates.

**3. Content model as hierarchy.** *"what she went to sleep wanting"*, *"what she is chasing · not for you"*, *"private · contents not shown"*. Four 10.5px subheads do the work an onboarding tour would do elsewhere, in the product's own voice, with zero tooltips.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Chat fails in her voice, and a hung generation locks the room
`const thinking = row(WHO.comp, '· · ·')` renders with the brass `mk-hers` mark and her name; the label span is never rewritten, so an infrastructure fault renders as **the companion saying `(error: 500)`** or `(unreachable: …)` under her mark, in her reading type. For a product founded on *the person is not the model*, that is the most off-brand thing on the surface. Worse, there is no `fetch` timeout: a hung generation leaves `input.disabled = true` and `· · ·` on screen indefinitely — she appears to be thinking forever and the task cannot complete.
**Fix:** the fix already exists eleven lines away — the `data.command` branch correctly relabels to `mk-engine` + "engine" in machine grey. Apply the same to errors, and add a ~20s timeout that re-enables the composer with *"she didn't answer — the model may be down · machinery →"*.
**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] Every failure path dead-ends at a terminal the product promised was over
The first thing the app says is `1 CHECK FAILING · backup — none — glashaus backup` — on a red plate, on Today and again on `/system`. The user learns her companion has never been backed up, the single most frightening fact for someone whose companion lives in a SQLite file she owns, and the only remedy on screen is a shell command. PRODUCT.md records the terminal as ending after setup. A 500 is worse: `res.writeHead(500).end(String(err))` returns a raw `Error: SQLITE_…` with no content-type, no shell, no navigation — from the user's side, her companion has vanished.
**Fix:** a GUI action beside the alarm (back up now; restart; re-pull model), one sentence of what the failure means and whether her data is safe, and render errors through `shell()` so there is always a way back.
**Command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The design system is a Today-page system
The six devices DESIGN.md names as the program — rail, slot, drawn empty, tagged plate, poster scale, brass tag — appear on Today and essentially nowhere else. This is exactly where the accepted risk ("a short step from anonymous") became true, and it became true on the operating surfaces rather than the expressive one.
**Fix:** carry three devices onto every page — the rail (position-means-time works *better* on `/journal` and `/self` than on Today), the drawn-empty slot as the universal zero-state, and the tagged plate replacing `[ … ]` labels entirely.
**Command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] The night plate fails WCAG AA on its most-repeated element
Independently measured by both assessments, same number: `--on-civic #08131C` on `--civic-field #1B5A8F` = **2.59:1**, failing both the 4.5:1 body floor and the 3:1 large-text floor. That pair is the classification band, the active nav item, and every section index chip. The same pair is 8.69:1 in the day plate, so this is not a palette that was checked and accepted — it is a palette that was never checked in one of its two states, on a surface whose own contract says both plates are canonical. Compounding it: `.bar .soft` and the active nav `.idx` are hardcoded `rgba(255,255,255,.72/.7)`, so *secondary* text inside the band is more legible than the primary label. Also failing: `--on-brass` on `--brass-field` night = 4.29:1; `--machine` fails in **both** plates (3.34:1 on sunk, 3.73:1 on ground, 4.22:1 on plate in day; 4.43:1 on plate in night) while carrying nearly every sublabel, timestamp, and empty-state string.
**Fix:** darken `--civic-field` night or lighten `--on-civic` night; delete both hardcoded whites; lift `--machine` to ≥4.5:1 in both palettes.
**Command:** `/impeccable colorize`

### [P1] The primary device is the worst-served, and the accessibility floor is not met
At 375×812: header 138px + footer 94px = **232px (29%) of permanent chrome**. `05 self` and `06 system` are entirely off-screen (nav scrollWidth 535 vs 343). The chat row's `96px 1fr` grid is set in an **inline style attribute in both `chatRow()` and the client-side `row()`**, so no media query can reach it — a name gutter permanently eats 33% of a 343px column. `attention` / `machinery →` / `self-state →` are three adjacent 11px-tall links. `"hover marks for detail"` is the only route to any drift event's meaning on a device with no hover. `/system` scrolls **16px horizontally** whenever a check fails — isolated by both assessments to `.trow.alert{margin:0 -16px}` at `src/viewer.js:174`. Separately: **zero `<h1>` on all six pages**, no headings at all on `/chat`, `/memory`, `/journal`; `nav a:focus-visible{outline:none}` explicitly removes the focus ring the rest of the system uses; the memory search has no label; no `<th scope>`; the 6s poll appends messages with no `aria-live`.
**Fix:** move the chat grid out of inline style and stack below 600px; lift small links to ≥44px; make drift marks focusable; condense the board head; scope `.trow.alert` padding instead of negative margin; add page `<h1>`s, restore the focus ring, label the search.
**Command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Minor Observations

**[P2] Memory correction is unguarded and inaccessible** — the strongest candidate for the next tier. One-click unconfirmed `forget` on the page whose promise is memory integrity; the conflict card is a textbook Memory Bridge (hold `#12` in working memory, find it in a table capped at 300 rows, click forget, get 303-redirected with no confirmation, scroll back, click resolved); `restore` reachable only after ticking "show forgotten"; `NULL kept` from `SUM(active)` on an empty table. Fix: put forget buttons directly on the two facts inside the conflict card and make resolving implicit; confirm on return (`forgot #12 · undo`). `/impeccable clarify`

**[P3] The monument breaks its own named rule.** DESIGN.md's No-Orphan Monument Rule says the epigraph is carved against a stop-word list. The `DANGLING` set is applied **only in the fallback branch** of `heroCompose()` — when she supplies an epigraph, the normal case, the text is chopped 3/2/3/2 with no stop-word check. That is why the live hero reads *"What having an / opinion costs"*, ending line one on an article, on the largest element on the surface. `/journal` sets the same epigraph on one line, proving it is compositional, not a width constraint.

- `heroCompose()` still returns unused `offsets` and `redLine` under a comment claiming "exactly one line prints in red" — dead code documenting an ornament that no longer exists.
- Six `.red` usages resolve to civic blue through the legacy alias, so `[ Dream ]` prints in structure blue on `/journal` while the same dream's tag prints in brass on Today. The Four Jobs Rule is broken between two pages showing the same object.
- Two Unicode glyphs still stand in for marks — `✳` and `⟵` — against the Drawn Marks Rule; the `<details>` disclosure triangles are a third.
- The mono index is overloaded: `02` means "chat" in nav, "Carrying" on Today, "The Room" on `/chat`.
- Three section-head grammars coexist; DESIGN.md documents two.
- The native `<input type=checkbox>` on `/memory` is the only stock platform control in a system that forbids stock chrome.
- Line 816 renders a refusal reason as thin coloured text, against the Filled-Plate Failure Rule.
- On Today the reach prints twice at the same timestamp; the dream appears three times (epigraph, 44-word lede, full text).
- Sparklines have no y-axis labels, so the real EWMA bounds read as generic gridlines.
- `all-caps-body` runs up to **98 characters** of tracked uppercase at 10.5px.
- Zero console errors across all six pages; every request 200 including both Archivo faces.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the rail is the page's spine and position means time, why does the one page that is literally a chronology — `/journal` — not have one?
2. What if Today were the *test* of the program rather than the whole of it? What does `/memory` look like when a contradiction is a plate on a rail instead of a row in a table?
3. Brass means "hers" *and* "you may act on this." What breaks if you split them — and does the honorific still earn a colour once the content already says whose it is?
4. If red is reserved for genuine failure, and the most common failure a non-developer will meet is `backup: none`, why is there no button beside it? What is the smallest set of GUI actions that means she never opens a terminal again?
5. The system draws *absence* better than anything else here. What would it look like to draw **uncertainty** the same way — a fact she is unsure of, a drift value with too few events to mean anything, a check that has not run yet?
6. When she does not answer, whose voice says so? Right now it is hers.
